# Lite Paper

## Abstract
**OctoLend** is a curated, modular lending protocol designed for institutions and advanced DeFi users. It combines **isolated, immutable markets** with **collateral delegation** and **configurable interest-rate models** (including fixed-rate borrowing). The system is built from three composable primitives—Earn Vaults, Market Vaults, and Collateral Vaults—that let curators construct capital-efficient markets for RWA collateral, fixed-rate credit lines, DeFi looping, and delta-neutral strategies. 

## 1) Motivation
On-chain credit still struggles to serve two ends of the spectrum simultaneously: (1) institutions that require predictable borrowing, real world mirroring guarantees, and RWA support; and (2) LPs who demand composability, isolated risk, and attractive risk-adjusted returns. OctoLend targets this overlap: institutional-grade structures without sacrificing the modularity and openness that drive on-chain financial innovation.

## 2) Design Principles
- Curated vault: Curators can operate Earn Vaults and deploy verified markets.
- Isolated market: Anyone can create isolated markets; Once deployed, parameters and oracle cannot be changed, reducing governance and parameter risk.
- Modular: Credit, collateral, rates, and incentives are discrete modules—swappable without rewriting the system.
- RWA-ready: Collateral delegation and fixed rates mirror real-world facilities while staying fully on-chain and non-custodial.

## 3) System Architecture

### 3.1 Primitives
- Market Vaults (the credit engine): Single-asset lending markets that escrow collateral, apply a chosen interest-rate model, and expose read-only DebtTokens for transparency.  
- Collateral Vaults (the underwriting rail): Immutable vaults whose deposits can be delegated to named borrowers—separating collateral provision from loan origination and decentralizing underwriting.  
- Earn Vaults (the curated aggregator): Curator-managed vaults that allocate deposits across selected markets (and other yield sources) to deliver diversified, risk-managed returns.

## 4) Capital Efficiency and Safety

### 4.1 Collateral Delegation
Borrowers can receive delegated collateral from Collateral Vaults, increasing borrowing power without rehypothecating posted assets. Delegation remains opt-in, paid, and revocable by policy, spreading risk and income between depositors (delegators), curators, and end-borrowers—useful for market-makers, structured credit, and programmatic facilities.

### 4.2 Isolated, Immutable Markets
Each Market is a self-contained risk vault. Immutable parameters reduce governance shocks and simplify risk modeling for institutions. Supply/borrow caps, LTV/LLTV buffers, and market-specific liquidation discounts create predictable boundaries for behavior under stress.

## 5) Interest Rate Models (IRMs)

### 5.1 Utilization-Driven (Dynamic Kink / PI Controller)
Markets can define an optimal utilization band with stable rates. Outside it, rates adjust block-by-block—rising to attract liquidity or prompt repayment. This creates a self-balancing market with automated liquidity restoration at high utilization.

### 5.2 Linear Kink
Rates increase linearly with utilization up to a critical threshold, where they steepen to defend liquidity for depositors.

### 5.3 Fixed-Rate Borrowing
For facilities that require predictability (e.g., RWA or treasury use cases), an IRM can be configured to a true fixed rate across all utilization levels, enabling on-chain analogs of term sheets and credit lines.

## 6) Oracles & Pricing
- Oracle-agnostic interface: Markets consume standardized functions (e.g., lastprice, decimals under SEP-40-style norms).  
- One-shot selection: Oracles are fixed at market creation, pushing rigorous diligence up-front and avoiding mid-lifecycle oracle risk.

## 7) Liquidations & Loss Handling
- Predictable triggers: Positions become liquidatable once risk-adjusted collateral falls below debt.  
- Configurable incentives: Market creators set max discounts; an optional cool-off reduces reflexive liquidations immediately after opening a borrow.  
- Bad debt socialization: Residual shortfall, if any, is socialized across depositors within that market only, preserving isolation across the protocol.  
- Bot-friendly: Profitable operations incentivize third-party keepers to continuously stabilize markets.

## 8) Institutional Features

### 8.1 RWA Collateral
When RWA collateral such as tokenized MMFs, treasuries, receivables, or other RWAs first come on-chain their utility and liquidity might be limited, making them less attractive for lending markets. Collateral delegation (e.g. XLM) could help bootstrap RWA collateral by acting as a first loss in liquidation events.

### 8.2 Credible Financial Guarantee
The combination of isolated markets, collateral delegation (including RWAs), no collateral rehypothecation, and curated Earn Vaults creates a transparent trail of responsibility and control—key for institutional adoption.

### 8.3 Withdrawal Predictability
Utilization-responsive IRMs (for variable markets) push rates aggressively at high usage to restore liquidity; fixed-rate markets can be paired with conservative reserve and buffers to keep redemption profiles within policy.

## 9) Economics & Incentives

### 9.1 Fees
By default, 10% of borrowing interest routes as protocol fees: 5% to OctoDAO and 5% to vault curator. Fees accrue in vault shares, compounding until withdrawn.

### 9.2 Fee Flow (Auctions)
An open module that auctions accrued fees (in underlying assets or vault shares) for OCTO via reverse-Dutch auctions. Proceeds flow to the DAO treasury for burn, distribution, or growth.

### 9.3 Reward Streams (Permissionless Emissions)
Projects can stream multiple reward tokens simultaneously to suppliers and/or borrowers—without staking or locking vault shares—so users can earn and borrow at once, enabling targeted growth without fragmenting liquidity.

### 9.4 OCTO Token
OCTO aligns curators, markets, and users, bootstraps early adoption, and can serve as an emergency backstop in extreme bad-debt scenarios subject to governance.

## 10) Governance & Operations
- Curator verification for Earn Vaults: KYB curators publish mandates, parameters, and rebalancing rules; optional timelocks create user reaction windows.  
- Market-level minimalism: With immutability and isolation, market governance is pre-determined (design diligence) rather than ongoing (tuning churn).  
- Transparency: DebtTokens and on-chain accounting expose liabilities, fees, and rewards to explorers and analysts.

## 11) User Journeys
- Passive depositor (Earn Vault): Deposit into a curator’s mandate (e.g., USDyc) → receive diversified exposure to several Markets → benefit from curator risk controls, fees compounding, and optional reward streams.  
- Institutional borrower (Fixed-Rate): Negotiate a fixed-rate market with predefined caps, LTVs, and an approved oracle → receive collateral delegation from a Collateral Vault → borrow at a predictable rate with immutable terms.  
- Curator: Create Earn Vault to attract and manage LP deposits with a transparent mandate. Earn Vaults can allocate to lending markets as well as any other yield sources; deploy markets for specific collateral or borrower needs.

## 12) Conclusion
OctoLend delivers institutional-grade lending with DeFi-native modularity. Its blend of immutable, isolated markets, collateral delegation, configurable IRMs (including fixed-rate), and oracle-agnostic design gives curators a precise toolkit to build predictable, capital-efficient credit, without sacrificing permissionless access or composability. OctoLend offers a pragmatic foundation for both conservative RWA credit and advanced on-chain strategies—under one architecture.
