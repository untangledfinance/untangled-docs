# Custody-preserving execution

You want an agent to put your treasury to work. You do not want to hand your
treasury to anyone. Custody-preserving execution is how both hold at once: your
funds stay in your own custody wallet - an MPC account such as Fordefi - and an
agent can move them only inside limits you set, and only in the ways you allowed.
Prime never holds your funds.

## Four gates, and three are yours

Every move the agent proposes passes four checks. You own three of them. Prime
runs the fourth, and it can only refuse a move, never widen what your three
allow.

- **Gate 1, yours - how much, and until when:** a spending limit on your custody
  account, with an amount and an end date. Nothing spends past the amount, and
  when the date passes everything stops until you set a new limit.
- **Gate 2, yours - where funds may go:** a custody gate you deploy and own. It
  holds the limit and the short list of addresses funds may be released to. It
  has no admin and no settings, so nobody can edit that list; changing it means
  deploying a new gate.
- **Gate 3, yours - who can change any of this:** your account's approval
  threshold. One key alone moves nothing. See [Signers and approvals](./04.signers-and-approvals.md).
- **Gate 4, ours - is this move inside the mandate:** the rule that reads each
  proposed move and checks it against what you allowed - the amount, the venue,
  the direction, and where the funds must return.

:::note
Prime's gate can refuse a move your gates would allow. It can never allow one
your gates would refuse. Adding Prime makes fewer things possible with your
funds, never more.
:::

## Set up a custody gate

The custody gate is your contract, not Prime's: your wallet deploys it, and your
custody wallet grants it the limits. Prime never appears as the source of the
funds or as an authoriser. When an account has no gate yet, Prime offers a short
guided flow under **Policies**, in four steps:

- **Custody:** the wallet that holds the funds and grants the limit. The gate
  spends from this account and never holds the funds itself.
- **Destinations:** the Prime Account this gate serves, and the addresses its
  funds may reach. The account's execution adapter is the only caller the gate
  answers to, and the first address it may release to. Most gates need nothing
  more.
- **Limit:** the assets the gate may draw, and for each one how much it may
  release before you grant again and how many days that lasts. The gate refuses
  any asset you did not list.
- **Review:** the gate address, what it spends from, what it releases to, and
  what it refuses, all shown before anything is signed.

![The custody gate wizard: choosing the account that holds the funds, with the four steps across the top](/img/prime/custody-gate-setup.png)

Setting up a gate takes one transaction to deploy it and one more to grant each
asset's limit, all signed by your custody wallet. If that wallet needs more than
one signature, each transaction waits in
[Transactions - Queue](./06.transactions-and-co-signing.md) for the other
signers. A gate's destinations and assets are fixed when it is created; changing
either means deploying another gate. Raising, lowering, or revoking a limit is a
new grant from your custody wallet, and needs no one else.

## Give an agent a mandate

A mandate is the rule that says what the agent may do with the gated funds. You
set it from **Let an agent move custody's money**, and you choose:

- **What it may do:** supply to or withdraw from a pool, or swap on one.
- **The asset and the direction.**
- **An amount band:** the lowest and highest a single move may be, and how many
  approvals that band needs. A larger band with a higher approval count is a
  separate rule, so you can let small moves run alone and hold large ones for a
  second approval.
- **For a swap, the least it must return to custody**, so a move cannot trade at
  a bad price.

![The mandate dialog: what an agent may do, the pool, the amount band, and the return floor](/img/prime/custody-mandate.png)

Each band installs as its own rule, and the dialog reads the chain to show what
the account already carries, so you can add one band today and another later.

## How one move happens

1. You set the spending limit and deploy your gate. This uses your keys only, and
   you do it once.
2. The agent proposes a move.
3. Prime's rule reads the move and checks it against the mandate. If the amount,
   the venue, or the destination is wrong, it refuses and nothing moves.
4. If it passes, your gate releases the funds - never more than the limit, and
   only to a listed address.
5. The funds are put to work at the venue, and when the position is closed they
   return to your account.

Steps 2 to 5 happen inside one transaction. If any part fails, every part
reverses and the funds are back where they started. There is no state where half
the move has happened.

## Where to start

A good first mandate is small and tightly bounded - a modest supply to a single
lending pool is a calm way to watch the controls work on real value before
anything larger is delegated. Start with a spending limit small enough that
losing it would not matter, and widen only by agreement.

## What is proven, and what is not

:::caution
Custody-preserving execution runs on Stellar's test network today. Nothing here
is deployed for real money yet. The contracts carry the team's own tests and
internal review, not an outside audit. The lending-pool round trip, into a Blend
pool and back, is proven end to end on the network; the swap path is built but
has not been run against a live venue.
:::

While a position is open, the venue records it in the Prime Account's name, not
your custody wallet's - crediting the custody wallet directly would need its
approvals on every step, which is the friction this design removes. The mandate
fixes the way out to your account: a withdrawal aimed anywhere else is refused.
