# FAQ

### What is a Prime Account?

Your own smart account on Stellar, under rules you set: who can approve, and how
many must agree. It acts only when those rules are met - the right signers have
approved, and every call is within the policies that cover it. Your funds stay in
your custody wallet; a Prime Account is not a custodian.

### Do I need a new wallet?

No. You connect a Stellar wallet you already have, such as Freighter. That wallet
becomes the first signer of your Prime Account and signs on its behalf.

### What does it cost to use?

You pay Stellar network fees, in XLM, for the transactions you sign. Setup is two
of them - one to deploy the account and one to install your signers and threshold.
Later actions cost network fees the same way: changing signers is two
transactions, installing a policy is one, and a Blend execution template takes one
transaction to activate the adapter the first time plus several to apply the
policy. Keep a small amount of XLM in your wallet to cover them.

### Can I change my signers or threshold later?

Yes. Both are managed under the Signers tab. A change is two transactions and is
approved by the account like any other. See
[Signers and approvals](./04.signers-and-approvals.md).

### What happens if I lose one of my keys?

On a shared account, the threshold is what protects you. If you set two of three,
losing one key still leaves two that can act, and you can remove the lost key and
add a new one. Set your threshold so that losing a single key does not lock you
out or hand control to whoever holds it.

### Can an agent I delegate to take more than I allowed?

Only within the policy you gave it. A delegated key acts under the rule it sits
on and a call outside that rule is refused. Read
[What a policy does not protect](./09.security.md#what-a-policy-does-not-protect)
before you delegate, because a key placed on more than one rule is bounded only by
the loosest one.

### Is Prime audited?

The policy interpreter is ours and its build is published. Some supporting pieces
are OpenZeppelin example contracts we deployed ourselves and have not audited. See
[Third-party components](./09.security.md#third-party-components).

### What if the app is unavailable?

Your funds are never trapped by the app. They stay in your custody wallet, and a
Prime Account is a contract on Stellar that exists independently of the app - the
signers are your own wallets and the rules live on chain. The app is a
convenience for reading and building transactions, not custody. The interpreter's
addresses and build hash are published, so the account can be read and operated
with other tools if you need to.

### Does Prime work across chains?

Prime runs on Stellar today. A cross-chain surface, PrimeX, is in development.
