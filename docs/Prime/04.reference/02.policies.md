# Policies

A policy is a rule the account checks before it approves a call. If the call
breaks the rule, the account refuses and nothing moves. Policies let you hand a
key to an agent, a service or a colleague and limit what it can do, instead of
trusting it to behave.

The **Policies** page has two tabs on the same rules. **Policies** shows what is
allowed: the account's rules and any policies you added. **Signers** shows who
can act. A permission made of several rules shows as one row you can expand, and
**View** opens a rule's full detail. The templates are below the list.

## Templates

| Template | What it permits | You provide |
|---|---|---|
| Custody-funded action | Supply, withdraw or swap with funds drawn from custody through the custody gate, within an amount band, returned to custody | The move, the pool, the direction, an amount band, a minimum wait, who may act and how many must approve, and for a swap a return floor. See [Install rules for an agent](../03.walkthrough/04.install-rules-for-an-agent.md). |
| Aqua swap | Swap on one Aquarius pool, one direction, capped per swap | The signer, the pool, which token goes in and out, and a cap per swap |
| Spending limit | Transfers through one asset contract, capped over a window of ledgers | The signer, the asset, a cap and a window in ledgers |
| Blend supply | Supply from your custody wallet to a Blend pool, with the position kept in this account | The signer, the Blend pool, the reserve asset and a cap per supply |
| Blend withdraw | Withdraw one Blend reserve to your custody wallet | The signer, the Blend pool, the reserve asset and a cap per withdrawal |
| Blend claim to wallet | Claim a Blend pool's emissions, paid straight to a wallet | The signer, the Blend pool and the wallet to pay |
| Pull funds | Pull an asset from the connected wallet into this account, capped per pull, sendable only to one address | The signer, the wallet to pull from, the asset, a cap per pull and the destination |

Every template also takes a name for the rule. If none fits, **Add a custom
policy** lets you build the rule yourself.

For an agent that should use custody's money, start from **Custody-funded
action**. It draws through the custody gate, takes a minimum wait for the time
lock, and is the template the walkthrough uses.

The Aqua swap template can route its swaps through the account's execution
contract, which is what makes its per-swap cap hold. Prime Execution in the app
does not run those routed swaps, so pick that option only for an agent that
submits its own batches.

## What a policy can limit

| You want to say | How |
|---|---|
| Only this contract | The rule is scoped to one contract |
| Only this method | The policy pins the method name |
| At most this much per call | A cap on the amount |
| Only these recipients | A list of allowed recipients |
| At most this much per day or month in total | A spending limit beside the policy |
| A trade must return at least a set minimum | A floor comparing what a trade spends with what it returns |

Two more limits work on chain but are not in the app yet: several approvals for
one delegated action, and an expiry after which a rule stops working.

A spending limit caps how much, not how often, and it only counts transfers. A
limit such as "at most ten calls a day" cannot be expressed today; we
[opened an issue with OpenZeppelin](https://github.com/OpenZeppelin/stellar-contracts/issues/858)
about it.

## Install a policy

1. Pick a template and fill in its addresses and caps, or select **Add a custom
   policy**.
2. The app shows a review in plain words: which contract, which method, which
   cap, which recipients.
3. Your wallet shows the transaction and you sign it. Nothing is installed until
   you sign, so if the review does not say what you meant, stop there.
4. If the account needs more approvals than yours, the other signers co-sign it
   from the Queue.

If the install button is disabled, the app says why beside it, for example that
the signer you chose already holds a rule that would conflict, and what to
change.

## Revoke a policy

Revoking removes the policy from its rule. The app builds the revoke transaction
the same way, and your wallet signs it. Removing a policy also resets the
counters it kept, such as a spending limit's total for the period.

Some permissions are several rules that only work together, such as a policy and
the rule that lets it run as a batch. The Policies tab groups them into one row.
Removing any one of them stops the whole permission, and the others stay listed
until you remove them too. The confirm dialog warns you when a policy may belong
to such a group.

## What a policy does not protect

Read these before you rely on a delegated key. [Security](../05.security.md)
covers each in full.

| Limit | What to do |
|---|---|
| A key is only as limited as the loosest rule it sits on | Put a delegated key on its own rule and nowhere else. |
| A second signer on a rule with a policy does not mean two approvals: either can act alone | For two approvals, set the approval count on a custody-funded action band, as in the walkthrough. |
| The account's own signers are not limited by a policy | That is what lets them revoke it. |
| Changing the signers on a rule stops its policy working | Install the rule again after the change. |

The [OctoGate user guide](../../OctoGate/account-permissioning/user-guide.md)
has recordings of three policies being allowed and then refused on testnet: a
daily claim limit, a monthly subscription cap and a swap floor.
