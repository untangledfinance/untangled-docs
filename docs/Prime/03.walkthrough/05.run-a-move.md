# Run a move

These steps are for rules without a wait. For rules with a wait, see
[Use the time lock](./06.use-the-time-lock.md).

## Build and run a batch

1. Connect the agent's wallet, choose the Prime Account with the **Prime
   Account** button at the top of the page, and go to **Apps**, then **Prime
   Execution**.
2. Under **Authorised by**, choose the rule to use.
3. Add the calls. You can fill in one card per call in the **Interactive** view,
   or paste the whole batch in the **Raw** view. A batch can have up to eight
   calls.
4. Leave **Wait before it runs (ledgers)** at its default (0 in the video).
5. Select **Execute**. The app simulates the batch and shows what the account
   would authorise and the estimated fee.
6. Select **Run** and sign.

A swap of custody's XLM on Aquarius is three calls: the gate pulls the XLM from
custody, the pool swaps it with a minimum USDC amount, and the USDC is
transferred back to custody.

![Prime Execution with a three-call swap](/img/prime/prime-execution-calls.webp)

A batch can't call the Prime Account, the policy interpreter that checks its
rules, or the execution contract.

## If the batch breaks the rule

The app warns you before you sign. In the video the agent tries to swap 50 XLM
under the under-10 XLM rule, and a red line under the calls says the account
will refuse it.

![The warning before signing](/img/prime/prime-execution-warning.webp)

If you run it anyway, the simulation fails with **Refused** and nothing is
signed or sent. The warning comes from the app's simulation; the account
contract enforces the rule on chain either way.

![The refused batch](/img/prime/prime-execution-refused.webp)

## If the rule needs a second approval

After the agent signs, the app shows **More signatures needed** and saves the
request in the Queue. The other approver, the Admin in the video, opens the
co-sign link from **Transactions**, then **Queue**, checks each call and selects
**Sign and continue**. The swap goes through once they sign.
