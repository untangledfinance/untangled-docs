# Policies

A policy is a rule your account checks before it authorises a call. If the call
does not satisfy the rule, the account refuses and nothing moves. You manage
policies under the **Policies** tab.

Policies are how you delegate without trust. You can hand a key to an agent, a
service, or a colleague, and bound what that key is able to do, instead of
relying on it to behave.

The Policies tab is one of two lenses on the same rules: **Signers** asks who can
act, and **Policies** asks what is allowed. It lists the account's rules - the
approval team and any policies you have added. A permission made of more than one
rule shows as a single row you can expand to see the rules it runs, and **View**
opens a rule's full detail. Templates to start from are below the list.

## Start from a template

A template sets up a common policy for you. Fill in the inputs and it produces
the rule. Every template also takes a name for the rule.

| Template | What it permits | You provide |
|---|---|---|
| Aqua swap | Swap on one Aquarius pool, one direction, capped per swap | The signer key, the pool, which token goes in and out, and a cap per swap |
| Spending limit | Cap transfers through one asset contract over a ledger window | The signer key, the asset, a cap, and a window in ledgers |
| Blend supply | Supply from your custody wallet to a Blend pool, keeping the position in this account | The signer key, the Blend pool, the reserve asset, and a cap per supply |
| Blend withdraw | Withdraw one Blend reserve to your custody wallet | The signer key, the Blend pool, the reserve asset, and a cap per withdrawal |
| Blend claim to wallet | Claim accrued emissions from one Blend pool, paid straight to a wallet | The signer key, the Blend pool, and the wallet emissions are paid to |
| Pull funds | Pull an asset from the connected wallet into this account, capped per pull, to one allowed destination | The signer key, the wallet to pull from, the asset, a cap per pull, and the destination |
| Custody-funded action | Let an agent supply, withdraw, or swap using funds drawn from your custody wallet through a gate, capped per move and returned to your account | The action, the pool, the asset and direction, an amount band and its approvals, and for a swap a return floor |

If none of these fit, **Add a custom policy** lets you build the rule yourself.

The Custody-funded action draws from your own custody wallet under a gate and
needs one set up first; see [Custody-preserving execution](./08.custody-preserving-execution.md).

The Aqua swap template's per-swap cap can only be enforced when the swap is
routed through the account's execution adapter, and the app can no longer run
those routed swaps itself. For a capped swap you can actually run, use the
Custody-funded action instead. See [Apps and execution](./07.apps-and-execution.md).

## What you can bound

| You want to say | How |
|---|---|
| Only this contract | The rule is scoped to one contract |
| Only this method | The policy pins the method name |
| At most X per call | A cap on the amount |
| Only these recipients | An allowlist on the recipient |
| At most X per day or month in total | A spending limit alongside the policy |
| A trade must return at least a set minimum | A slippage floor comparing what a trade spends to what it returns |

Two more constraints - requiring several approvals for one delegated action, and
an expiry that stops a rule after a set time - are supported on chain but not yet
offered in the app.

A total-spend cap bounds how much, not how often, and it meters transfers. A
limit of "at most ten calls a day" is not expressible today; we have opened an
issue with OpenZeppelin
[here](https://github.com/OpenZeppelin/stellar-contracts/issues/858).

## Installing a policy

1. Pick a template and fill in its addresses and caps, or select **Add a custom
   policy** to build the rule yourself.
2. The app shows you a review in plain language: which contract, which method,
   which cap, which recipients.
3. It produces an unsigned transaction.
4. Your wallet shows it to you and you sign it. That signature is the
   confirmation step; nothing is installed until you sign.

If the review card does not say what you meant, do not sign. Nothing has happened
yet.

If the install button is disabled, the app says why beside it - for example, the
signer you chose already holds a rule that would conflict - and what to change to
clear it.

## Revoking a policy

Revoking removes the policy from the rule. The app produces an unsigned revoke
transaction the same way, and your wallet signs it. Removing a policy also resets
any counters it was keeping, including a spending cap's running total for the
period.

A permission routed through the execution adapter is more than one rule: the
policy you reviewed, the adapter rule that lets it run as a batch, and for an
Aqua swap a separate spending cap, and they only work together. The Policies tab groups them into one row you can expand to
see the rules it runs, with a count of how many there are. Removing any one of
them stops the whole permission from working, and the others stay listed until
you remove them too. When a policy may belong to a set, the confirm dialog tells
you before you revoke.

## Worked examples

Each policy below was installed on Stellar testnet and both its permit and its
refusal were exercised. In each recording the policy is asked for in plain
English, the transaction is signed, and the result is read back on a block
explorer. Every transaction hash in the recordings is real.

### Delegating a yield claim

You hold a position that pays yield and you want an agent to claim it for you,
without being able to drain the account.

- **Rule:** the agent key may call `transfer` on XLM only, and no more than 15.3
  XLM per day in total.
- **What happened:** a 15.3 XLM claim was permitted. The same claim, repeated,
  was refused - the day's allowance was already spent.

### A subscription that bills you

A merchant needs to charge you monthly, and you want a hard ceiling on what they
can take.

- **Rule:** the merchant key may call `transfer` on one token only, and no more
  than 100 USDC per month in total.
- **What happened:** a 100 USDC charge was permitted. The identical charge,
  repeated, was refused.

### A bounded swap

You want an agent to trade for you, but never at a bad price.

- **Rule:** the agent may call the swap method on one router, and the trade must
  return at least a set minimum.
- **What happened:** a swap selling 10 XLM and demanding at least 1.5 USDC back
  was permitted and executed. The same swap, with the agent willing to accept one
  stroop less, was refused and never reached the venue. The floor is compared
  against the trade's own size, so one rule covers every trade amount.

## What a policy does not protect

A few limits are worth knowing before you rely on a delegated key. Each is
covered in full in [Security](./10.security.md).

- **A key is only as constrained as the loosest rule it sits on.** Put a
  delegated key on the policed rule and nowhere else.
- **Adding a second signer to a policed rule does not create dual approval.** Any
  one signer on the rule can act alone. Use a threshold policy for that -
  supported on chain, but not yet offered in the app.
- **The account owner is not constrained.** A policy bounds the keys you place
  under it; your own admin rule keeps full authority, which is what lets you
  revoke.
- **Changing signers stops the policy working.** Reinstall the rule after
  changing signers.
