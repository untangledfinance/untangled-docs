# Transactions and co-signing

Every move on your Prime Account is a transaction the account authorises against
its rules. **Transactions** has two tabs: **Queue**, for requests waiting on
approval, and **History**, for what has already happened.

![The Transactions tab, with an empty queue](/img/prime/transactions-tab.png)

Requests appear in the queue while Prime is open and an app is connected.

## When one signature is enough

If your account has a single signer, or the connected wallet alone meets the
threshold, a transaction is built, your wallet signs it, and it is submitted. The
app shows each step - build, simulate, sign, broadcast, confirm - as it happens.

## When it needs more approvals

If a transaction needs more approvals than the connected wallet can give, Prime
does not fail on chain. It produces a **co-signing link** that carries the
partly signed transaction to the other signers.

1. You start the transaction and sign your part.
2. Prime gives you a link to share with the other signers.
3. Each signer opens the link, reviews the transaction, and adds their signature.
4. Once the threshold is met, the transaction is submitted.

You can share the link however you like - a message, a short link, or by having a
signer paste the transaction into the co-signing screen directly. The request is
also saved in the Queue, so you can close the tab and come back to it.

![A co-signing link, showing who has signed and who is still needed](/img/prime/cosign-link.png)

## What a co-signer sees

When a signer opens a co-signing link, the app shows what is being signed by
decoding the transaction itself and reading the account fresh from the chain. It
does not trust anything the link says about itself.

A link that arrives over a message or a relayer cannot describe itself as one
call and carry another. A signer approves what the transaction actually does, not
what its sender claims. Addresses are shown in full so a signer can check them.

![A co-signer's review, showing the decoded call and the full addresses](/img/prime/cosign-review.png)

When the request is a batch run through the execution adapter, the review lists it
call by call: "Call 1", "Call 2", and so on, each naming the method and contract
it runs and what it lets the callee do as the adapter. You approve the whole batch
as one, having read every call it makes.

## Two people signing at once

If two signers open the same co-signing link together, the app holds a short lock
so their signatures do not collide. A signer who arrives while another is signing
is asked to wait a moment rather than sign over them.
