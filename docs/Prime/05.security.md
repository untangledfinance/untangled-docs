# Security

## How funds are protected

- Funds that rules move through the custody gate stay in your custody wallet. The gate
  draws on them through an allowance custody can reduce or revoke at any time.
- The gate can only pay the addresses listed when it was created. It has no admin.
- The account contract checks the approval count and the agent's rules on chain.
  The app can't bypass them.
- Moves under a rule with a wait show up in the Queue first, and custody or the
  account's signers can cancel them.
- If custody loses its keys, the Prime signers can send the funds to the gate's
  recovery address. At the account's full approval count they can also draw
  custody's funds to any other address on the gate's list, the Prime Account
  included, within custody's allowance. An agent's rules don't allow this.
- The co-sign page decodes the transaction itself and ignores any text sent
  with the link.
- A move that custody's allowance doesn't cover fails, whatever the Prime rules
  allow.

## Setting up policies

- Keep each delegated key on a single rule. The caller picks which rule to
  use, so a key that is also on a rule without a policy can act through that
  one.
- Use the approval count to require two people. In **Add a rule**, two listed
  signers with **Approvals required** at 1 can each act alone; at 2, both must
  sign.
- Policies apply to delegated keys. The account's own signers aren't
  restricted by them.
- Spending limit periods are in ledgers. A ledger closes roughly every five
  seconds, so a day is about 17,280 ledgers.
- Pin the method as well as the amount. A spending limit counts only calls to
  `transfer`.
- Reinstall a rule after changing its signers. Its policy refuses calls
  until you do (see
  [Signers and approvals](./04.reference/01.signers-and-approvals.md)).
- Spending limits cap the total amount, and the number of calls stays open. We have
  [raised call-frequency limits with OpenZeppelin](https://github.com/OpenZeppelin/stellar-contracts/issues/858).

## Third-party code

Spending limits and threshold approvals use OpenZeppelin's example contracts,
which OpenZeppelin publishes as experimental. The policy interpreter that
evaluates your rules is our own. The OctoGate page
[Prime contracts on Stellar](../OctoGate/prime-contracts.md#third-party-code)
says which code our review covered.

## Mainnet contracts

The addresses and code hashes of the Prime Account, the policy interpreter, the
custody gate, the execution contract and the OpenZeppelin policies on mainnet
are on the OctoGate page
[Prime contracts on Stellar](../OctoGate/prime-contracts.md#mainnet-contracts).
The app refuses a gate or execution contract that runs any other code.

## Audit status

modelling. An external audit is in progress.
