# Signers and approvals

Signers are the wallets allowed to act on your Prime Account. The approval
threshold is how many of them must agree before a transaction goes through. You
find both under **Policies**, on the **Signers** tab.

## Reading the current setup

The Signers tab lists every signer on the account and the threshold in force. A
line such as "a change needs 2 of 3 approvals" means the account holds three
signers and any two of them together can authorise a change.

Each signer shows how many rules it sits on. Your own wallet governs the account
and sits on more; a delegated key sits only on the rule that bounds it.

![The Signers tab for a two-of-three account](/img/prime/signers-tab.png)

## Changing signers or the threshold

Select **Manage signers** to add a signer, remove one, or change the threshold.
The dialog shows the current signers and the approvals required, and summarises
the change before you apply it.

![The Manage signers dialog](/img/prime/manage-signers.png)

### It takes two transactions

The chain cannot edit the account's rule in place, so a change is done in two
steps: the new team is installed first, then the old one is retired. Both steps
are shown as they happen. The second step needs the new team's approval, and it
may need different people than the first, so they are not hidden behind one
button.

If a step fails partway, you can see which of the two landed and continue.

### The new team approves the change

Changing the team is itself a transaction the account must authorise, so it goes
through approvals like any other. If the connected wallet cannot meet the
threshold alone, Prime produces a co-signing link for the other signers instead
of failing on chain. See [Transactions and co-signing](./06.transactions-and-co-signing.md).

This was checked on Stellar testnet on a two-of-two account: one signature was
refused, two were accepted, and a wallet that was not a signer was refused.

## After changing signers

If a key is delegated under a policy, changing the signers on that key's rule
stops the policy working until you reinstall it. This is a safety property, not a
bug: the policy refuses rather than apply to a different set of people than you
intended. See [Policies](./05.policies.md) for how to reinstall.
